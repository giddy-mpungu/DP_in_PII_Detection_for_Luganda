Luganda PII detector, trained with differential privacy

Type or paste Luganda text and see which words look like personal information. The detector is one of the models from an MSc study of differentially private (DP) PII detection. Everything runs in your browser: your text is never uploaded.

A research demo, not a redaction tool. Even the least private of these models misses about half of the PII mentions in held-out test text, and stronger privacy misses more (see how good is it). They were trained on lower-case Luganda news sentences, so other kinds of text will do worse. Always review the output yourself.

    Why the model is private

    Differential privacy is a property of how a model was trained, so it cannot be shown by looking at one prediction. What can be shown is the training recipe and its accounting, which anyone can check, plus an attack that fails.

    An attack that fails: membership inference

    An attacker tries to tell whether a sentence was in the training data from the model's loss on it. If training sentences (solid) have lower loss than unseen ones (dashed), the model has memorised them. AUC 0.5 means the attacker does no better than a coin flip.

    training sentences (members)   unseen test sentences

    This attack is weak, so it gives a lower bound on leakage, not proof of its absence. The proof is the certificate above; the attack shows the effect is real.

    What DP protects, and what it does not

    • Protected: the people in the 2,085 training sentences. Nothing the model does reveals much about any one of them.
    • Not covered by DP: the text you type here. It is protected differently: the model runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.
    • Not covered either: PII the detector misses in your text. That is a question of accuracy, below.
    • The guarantee is per sentence: a person who appears in k training sentences is protected at roughly kε.
